## Bottom line

Pass two closes the large coverage gap in pass one and finds one real source-data defect, three warranted metadata comments, one report defect, and several observations that should be cut. ### A1 — source attribution is sufficient, with one useful strengthening

The pass-two evidence was sufficient to show that the store references the changed upstream message, but its use of gribberish on both paths left a small same-decoder loophole: a packing-mode-specific codec bug could have reproduced itself in the raw-message and store reads. Message size alone would not close that loophole.

I independently inspected the two supplied GRIB records with `wgrib2`. The data-representation metadata changes exactly as the decoded grids imply:

```text
before: val=(28.515+i)*10^-3, 12 bits, range 0.028515..0.999515
after:  val=(3+i)*10^-2,       9 bits, range 0.03..1.00
```

Both use GRIB template 5.3; the decimal scale changed from three decimal places to two and the packed width fell from 12 to 9 bits. That is direct evidence that NCEP encoded the lower precision. The unchanged `SOILW` companion makes an archive-wide decoding change implausible. The source attribution is therefore established, not merely suggested.

The reader-visible defect is a source precision regression, not a reformatter defect. The archive should faithfully retain it. The right durable treatment is a review note for the bounded era, because this is exactly the repository's “source data changes over time” category. The note should state the precision and consequence, not narrate the verification.

### A2 — the mask recommendation fails the missing-value bar

The spatial association is persuasive: the near-one population is concentrated on permanent land ice, nearly static, and coincides with soil type near 16, zero vegetation, and constant field capacity. What is not established is the stronger claim that every value `>= 0.9` is invalid and no genuine soil value in the supported archive can reach that threshold.

The report tested the threshold at three times, then reclassified about 1,200 nominally non-ice cells as “ice margin” from the same interpolated soil-type field. That is circular evidence for a lossless threshold, not an independent truth mask. It also did not do the required materialized-decoder/virtual-codec comparison across supported source eras. Static maps and a one-year trace establish an intrinsic source/model convention; they do not establish a universal numeric missing marker.

My recommendation is comment-only metadata along the lines of: “Soil-moisture values are near 1 over permanent land ice.” Do not call the values measurements, but also do not call them an invalid sentinel without an authoritative source definition. Do not put A2 in Part B's masking category. If the owner wants a mask finding later, validate a permanent-ice selector independently across the archive; do not infer it from the moisture value being masked.

### A3 — not analogous enough to A1 to fix without the owner

At synoptic valid times, lead 6 from the preceding cycle is still a forecast for the correct valid time. It is therefore consistent with the existing “shortest available lead” construction even though it is not from the same model run as the lead-0 variables. That distinguishes A3 from the sunshine defect: A1 exposed inconsistent accumulation-window semantics, whereas A3 exposes a conflict between two plausible analysis contracts.

The precipitation inequality proves the cost is real. `instantaneous_precipitation_convective_surface` and `precipitation_rate_surface` cease to be a coherent decomposition at exactly the mixed-cycle positions. It does not by itself prove that the lead selection is wrong.

The owner needs to choose:

- **Coverage-first:** retain previous-cycle lead 6 and add evergreen comments to all five variables saying that 00/06/12/18 UTC values come from the preceding forecast cycle.
- **Coherence-first:** make those five values unavailable at synoptic hours, or define a broader same-cycle policy for coupled variables. NaN is more honest than silently mixing runs, but it deliberately discards a valid-time forecast.

Pass two overreaches by declaring the behavior settled and intrinsic. Comments encode the answer; they cannot substitute for making the contract choice.

### A4 — cut

The strongest deep fields carry 75–90% of their variation at cycle boundaries, not 100%, and only three land points in three short windows were tested. `vegetation_surface` is the one field shown to be entirely cycle-updated. The observations are credible and expected for an analysis assembled from successive forecast cycles, but “deep land-surface state updates only four times a day” is too categorical. Repeated hourly values and cycle-boundary steps are not missing, corrupt, or misleadingly encoded. No code, comment, owner escalation, or Part B bullet is warranted.

### A5 — keep only the actionable metadata fact

`no-rounding` establishes that the reformatter adds no mantissa rounding; it does not by itself prove the stated GRIB packing mechanism for every listed variable. The report did not inspect those packing headers, and “anything below 500 is indistinguishable from zero” assumes a rounding rule it did not establish.

The `ventilation_rate_planetary_boundary_layer` behavior is unusual enough to travel with the variable: source resolution alternates between roughly 100 and 1,000 m² s⁻¹, and zero is a valid quantized result, not a missing marker. Add that concise comment. The shear increments, ordinary 0.1 K/wind grids, and other listed resolutions are not findings worth permanent metadata.

### A6 — correction upheld, replacement wording narrowed

The core correction holds. The latitude-bin measurements directly contradict pass one: the signed PV surfaces are mostly absent in the opposite hemisphere's extratropics, while the deep tropics are about 95–99.8% present in the sampled cases. The global missing fraction was right and the geography was wrong.

Pass two's proposed Part B text still overgeneralizes. “93–100% absent south of 30°S” describes the +2 PVU examples, not all positive surfaces: +0.5 PVU is only 74.5–79.3% absent at 30–60°S in the shown samples. “Complete north of 10°N” also fails for +0.5 PVU in the winter polar cap, where 14.6% is absent at 60–90°N. The correct summary is qualitative and qualified by threshold and season.

What let the original sentence through—both in pass one and in my first review—was a failure to demand spatial evidence for a spatial claim. Pass one supplied a global fraction and a physically plausible story. I accepted the story while concentrating on whether the bullet belonged in Part B. More specifically, I conflated “PV magnitude tends toward zero near the equator” with “a particular signed PV isosurface fails to occur in the column.” Those are not the same statement.

I withdraw my blanket approval of the structural-unavailability geography in pass one. That does **not** mean the other bullets are now suspect to the same degree. Pass two supplied independent spatial support for them: exact land-mask agreement for the 13 land variables; moving sea-ice boundaries for the land-or-ice fields; terrain-following masks in the height group; stable enumerated pressure-level gaps; matched masks within cloud families; and ocean/high-terrain structure for Haines. Those can remain, with two refinements already needed on their own evidence: split ordinary cloud boundaries from the convective pair, and describe Haines as absent over ocean **and where 950/850 hPa lie below ground**. The lesson is to retain only geography backed by a map, latitude/terrain bin, or an independent mask—not to discard all structural findings.

### A7 — settled: both comments name the wrong band

The data ratio was only a clue; official code settles it. The GFS diagnostic maps all-sky and clear-sky UV outputs into the diagnostic fields, and RRTMG explicitly sets `nuvb = 27` and returns the downward flux for that single band. Band 27 spans 29,000–38,000 cm⁻¹, which converts to approximately **345–263 nm**. Thus the appropriate ascending wavelength description is **approximately 263–345 nm**, not 280–315 nm.

Change both variable comments. Keep “UV-B” if matching the NCEP field name is desired, but make clear that this is NCEP/RRTMG's broader diagnostic band rather than the conventional UV-B interval. Primary-code links: [GFS diagnostic mapping](https://github.com/NOAA-EMC/ufsatm/blob/develop/ccpp/driver/GFS_diagnostics.F90#L2778-L2811), [RRTMG band selection](https://github.com/ufs-community/ccpp-physics/blob/3e6660c6df54e95a0871e990c2294dd397ae3860/physics/Radiation/RRTMG/radsw_main.F90#L380), and [RRTMG band limits](https://github.com/ufs-community/ccpp-physics/blob/3e6660c6df54e95a0871e990c2294dd397ae3860/physics/Radiation/RRTMG/radsw_param.f#L182-L188).

### A8 — settled by the implementation, but phrase it accurately

The same-sign hemispheric distributions do not independently prove a convention; pass two was right to list that limitation. The official UPP calculation closes it. `CALHEL2` constructs the storm motion as 7.5 m s⁻¹ to the right of the 0–6 km mean shear and explicitly says it ignores the left mover, with no hemisphere branch. That is a global right-moving Bunkers-style solution. See the [official UPP implementation](https://github.com/NOAA-EMC/UPP/blob/b6972b286c78c97f53926818b66c0c6eef6e28c0/sorc/ncep_post.fd/CALHEL2.f#L313-L342).

Add an intrinsic comment saying exactly that. “Northern-Hemisphere convention globally” is an interpretation of the consequence and is less precise than the algorithmic fact a reader needs.

### A9 — observation valid, cause and scope overstated

The sampled upper-level trace values are real decoded values. The evidence does not establish that the GRIB packing reference created them; exact zeros at lower levels only show that the codec can represent zero. They could also be a model numerical floor. More importantly, four hydrometeors were sampled at four of 57 pressure levels and the model-level statement rests on two snapshots. Do not turn that into archive metadata or a mask rule.

The only action is on validation: plots pinned to 300 hPa cannot meaningfully review fields whose useful mass is lower in the column. Re-render them near 700–850 hPa. That is not a dataset finding and should not appear in Part B.

### A10 — no archive action, but not every rationale is equally strong

Keeping `total_cloud_cover_atmosphere` matches equivalent published variables and the repository's naming rule; a local rename in this unpublished dataset would create inconsistency rather than fix it. The 80% convective-cover maximum has no pile-up, and the pressure endpoints have no censoring pile-up. The temperature long/short-name asymmetry remains unexplained, but all three quantities retain correct units and `air_temperature`; a cosmetic consistency preference is not enough to change shared metadata. The official 2022 GFS index labels `PEVPR` as an instantaneous “3 hour fcst,” so its `step_type=instant` is correct.

The only pushback is rhetorical: “Every SUSPECT verdict ... is resolved” is too strong when some were merely judged harmless and the hydrometeor packing cause was not proven. That does not create a code or user-facing action.

## 3. The eight incomplete items

### Material limitations

1. **Unswept vertical levels:** substantive. It limits all claims about vertical-group content and could miss a level-specific mapping, packing, or availability defect. It particularly weakens A9's generalization.
2. **Element-wise source fidelity across the archive:** substantive for any global “the whole store is source-faithful” claim. It does not weaken A1 after the independent packing check, nor the spatial mask conclusions.
3. **Largest precipitation maxima not traced to source:** substantive but local. The 229–375 kg m⁻² values remain plausible, not source-confirmed. No proposed Part B conclusion depends on them.

### Non-material or now closed

4. **Availability/decode checks not rerun:** redundant, not a new weakness. Reusing pass one's exhaustive manifest result for the unchanged store is sound, and the 3,808 successful pass-two decodes corroborate it. It still does not prove every chunk decodes, so the wording should remain scoped.
5. **A5 packing headers not read:** cosmetic for the action once the unproven mechanism is removed from the wording. It prevents naming the mechanism, not reporting the observed resolution.
6. **A7 not checked against the source definition:** now closed by the RRTMG band table and diagnostic mapping.
7. **A8 not checked against a third source:** now closed by the official UPP calculation.
8. **Nine wind-v plots use different points/windows:** cosmetic. It prevents direct visual ladder comparison, but the spatial maps and numerical ladder checks support the conclusions.

The all-array drift sweep materially improves pass one, but it samples 14 positions/statistics; “no sign-consistent regime change anywhere” should still mean “none detected by this sweep,” not an exhaustive proof against short-lived or spatially localized changes.

### Patterns of unavailable data

- One or both GFS source products are unavailable at seven archive times: 2022-11-29 20:00 and 22:00 UTC, and 2022-11-30 02:00, 05:00, 09:00, 10:00, and 11:00 UTC. Affected variables are unavailable at those positions.

- Thirteen land-surface variables are unavailable over water: the four `volumetric_soil_moisture_*`, four `liquid_volumetric_soil_moisture_*`, `field_capacity_surface`, `wilting_point_surface`, `vegetation_surface`, `plant_canopy_surface_water_surface`, and `water_runoff_surface`.

- Eight variables are unavailable over open water but remain available over land and sea ice, so their coverage follows the seasonal ice edge: `snow_water_equivalent_surface`, `snow_thickness_surface`, the four `soil_temperature_*`, `ground_heat_flux_surface`, and `potential_evaporation_rate_surface`.

- `ice_temperature_surface` is unavailable wherever ice is absent, about 87% of the grid.

- The six `average_pressure_{high,middle,low}_cloud_{bottom,top}` and three `average_temperature_{high,middle,low}_cloud_top` variables are unavailable where the corresponding cloud layer is not diagnosed; depending on family, this affects about 18–41% of the grid.

- `pressure_convective_cloud_bottom` and `pressure_convective_cloud_top` are unavailable over about 75–78% of the grid and can be unavailable even where `convective_cloud_cover` is nonzero.

- The 48 variables on the signed ±0.5, ±1, ±1.5, and ±2 PVU surfaces are generally unavailable in the opposite hemisphere's extratropics: positive-PVU coverage is sparse in the Southern Hemisphere and negative-PVU coverage is sparse in the Northern Hemisphere. The deep tropics are mostly present in both signs; exact polar and midlatitude coverage varies with PVU threshold and season. About 31–40% of the global grid is unavailable.

- In `height_above_mean_sea_level`, all three variables are unavailable where terrain rises above the requested level: about 22.8% of the grid at 305 m, 13.8% at 914 m, 4.9% at 2,743 m, and 0.2% at 4,572 m.

- Eight `pressure_level` variables have fixed gaps in their level sets. `cloud_cover`, `cloud_mixing_ratio`, `graupel`, `ice_water_mixing_ratio`, `rain_mixing_ratio`, and `snow_mixing_ratio` are unavailable at the 18 levels from 40 to 0.01 hPa. `specific_humidity` and `ozone_mixing_ratio` are unavailable at 125, 175, 225, 275, 325, 375, 425, 475, 525, 575, 625, 675, 725, 775, 825, and 875 hPa.

- `haines_index_surface` is unavailable over ocean and where its 950/850 hPa input levels lie below terrain, including major ice sheets and high mountain regions; total unavailable coverage is about 58–60%.

### Variables the user must explicitly mask

- For `percent_frozen_precipitation_surface`, mask values below −0.1; they mark locations without precipitation.

- For `geopotential_height_cloud_ceiling`, mask values above 19,000 m; they represent the interpolated no-ceiling marker rather than a physical ceiling.

- Before temporally averaging `albedo_surface`, mask zero values; zero denotes an averaging window with no sunlight, not a physical albedo measurement.

### Source data changes over time

- From 2022-02-28 18:00 through 2022-07-03 06:00 UTC, `liquid_volumetric_soil_moisture_100_200cm` has 0.01 rather than 0.001 source precision. During this interval it can exceed `volumetric_soil_moisture_100_200cm` by up to about 0.010, so total-minus-liquid derivations can be slightly negative.
